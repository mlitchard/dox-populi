# About page — outline

**Title:** About Me
**Placement:** unnumbered — an about page, linked from the masthead and
every post. Its own structure; the post template does not apply.
**Tone law:** scope-honest. One path, no program, nothing for sale. No
bootcamp endorsement, ever.

## 1. Why this page exists

- The series assumes no CS degree — that's a promise about the reader.
  This page is the disclosure behind it: the author didn't take the
  credentialed path either. One paragraph; the roadmap's audience claim
  is not restated here.

## 2. The record, plain

Stated as facts, flat delivery. Order matters — the hardest-landing
fact goes last.

- Liberal arts dropout.
- Formal coursework: freshman calculus, a few C programming classes,
  ancient Greek, modern German.
- Self-taught: graph theory, Haskell.
- Entered tech on the sys admin / help desk / IT side.
- First software engineering job in his 40s, in a Perl shop — no
  interest in Perl, growth sacrificed to it, every ounce of social
  capital spent pushing Haskell into production.
- In the end, not loved in the Perl shop. (Keep it exactly that dry.
  Perl heebie-jeebies line available in the author's voice.)

## 3. The pattern

- The through-line, author-claimed: dragging rigor into rooms that
  didn't order it. Haskell into a Perl shop then; a machine-checked
  spec into a video game now.
- Draft-time candidate, author's to keep or kill: the difference this
  time — the checks back him up, and the record is public.
- The Perl-shop cost stays a cost. This section redeems the pattern
  without softening the price.

## 4. What this means, and where to start

- The point, in the author's words: you don't need to be a CS major to
  be successful.
- Scope honesty: one path, one person. Evidence the door isn't locked;
  no claim the room is easy.
- On the record: no bootcamp endorsement — nothing to enroll in,
  nothing for sale.
- The door: blog post zero, the ISO, the homework. Free.
