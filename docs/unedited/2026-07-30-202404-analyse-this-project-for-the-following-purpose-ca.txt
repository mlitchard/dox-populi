❯ expand on  hybrid approach talk about that more                               

● Aight, sit down, 'cause this is the part of the sermon where the two halves of
   the church stop fighting and start singing. The hybrid ain't a compromise —
  it's a division of labor that maps the search problem onto the cost structure.
   Dice where dice are enough. Semantics where semantics are required. Let me
  lay it out proper.

  The core insight: two different kinds of "mutation" hiding in one word

  Look at your own spec (dox/creeps.dox) and ask what "mutate the strategy" even
   means. It splits clean:

  Parametric mutation — desiredHarvesters: 1 → 2. harvesterBody: [work, carry, 
  move] → [work, work, carry, move]. deliveringSpawnFull.target: harvesting →
  delivering. These are dice rolls in a fixed box. No intelligence required —
  the box is already defined by the schema. An LLM doing this work is a surgeon
  flipping burgers.

  Structural mutation — "the harvester FSM needs a third state: queuing, for
  when the spawn is full but a delivery slot opens soon." "Split upgrader into
  upgrader + courier." "This organism keeps dying to raids; it needs a fleeing
  state wired to the hostileNear event." That's changing the shape of the box.
  Dice can't do this — there's no gene for a state that doesn't exist yet. Only
  a semantic mutator can, and it has to emit a correct .dox file when it does —
  which is exactly the job you already established the LLM is qualified for,
  because the checkers hold the shotgun.

  Tier 1: the inner loop — cheap, blind, relentless

  - Genome = data, not syntax. genome.json conforming to a schema: numeric genes
   (population targets), multiset genes (bodies, under an energy-cost cap),
  table genes (the transition matrix: (state, event) → state), tag genes (state 
  → action from the fixed action vocabulary).
  - Operators: point-mutate a number ±1; swap one transition target; add/drop a
  body part; crossover — splice mama's transition table with papa's body plan.
  Crossover is where GAs earn their keep, and it's trivial when the genome is a
  table.
  - Generator: schema-driven genome → .dox template expansion. Deterministic,
  milliseconds, no LLM call. Then paradox check + tsc against the frozen shell.
  Mutants that violate the Z3 obligations — body over budget, missing (state, 
  event) transition, orphan state — die free, before server time.
  - Cost: effectively zero per mutant. Population 50? Fine. Every generation?
  Fine. And each viable genome is a nix derivation — parallel builds, and
  identical genomes hit the cache. Elites carried between generations don't even
   rebuild.

  This loop does exploitation: local search, polishing whatever structure
  exists, finding the best numbers for the current shape.

  Tier 2: the outer loop — expensive, seeing, rare

  - Trigger — this is the discipline part. The LLM does NOT get called every
  generation. It gets called when the archive testifies to stasis: best fitness
  improved less than ε over the last K generations, or population diversity
  collapsed (everybody's converged on the same table). The orchestrator computes
   this from the score archive. No plateau, no call.
  - Inputs when called: the champion genome + its rendered .dox, the fitness
  history curve, and — this is the good part — telemetry autopsies. Memory.stats
   time series: where does energy flow stall? What event/state pairs do creeps
  sit in? In coevolution mode, the rival's telemetry too. The LLM isn't
  guessing; it's reading the coroner's report.
  - Outputs: M diverse structural variants (M, not 1 — I'll cross-examine why
  below), each as a schema extension + seed genomes in the new schema. New
  state, new role, new transitions — new genes. Then it hands the keys back to
  the dice: tier 1 explores the enlarged box for the next K generations.
  - Cost math, so you feel it: pop 20 × 100 generations pure-LLM = 2,000 calls,
  most of them doing arithmetic a dice roll does free. Hybrid with a plateau
  every ~10 gens = ~10 calls total, each one doing work only semantics can do.
  Two hundred times cheaper on the expensive operator, and the expensive
  operator is only ever doing expensive-operator work.

  The contract between tiers — where this lives or dies

  The genome schema is versioned and load-bearing. The deterministic generator
  must be schema-driven, not hardcoded — because tier 2's whole job is extending
   the schema. When the LLM adds a queuing state, the schema grows a row in the
  transition table and a tag gene, and the dumb generator must render it without
   being touched. Get this wrong and every LLM intervention means hand-editing
  the generator — human back in the loop, hybrid dead on arrival.

  And the boundary the LLM cannot cross: the shell's sensor/action vocabulary.
  Structural mutations compose new FSMs over fixed events × fixed actions. The
  LLM can invent fleeing; it cannot invent hostileNear — that's an observation,
  and observations are hands, not brain. New senses are a human decision, made
  rarely, deliberately, like evolution getting a new organ. Fixed embodiment,
  evolving mind — the brain/hands split isn't just compatible with the hybrid,
  it's what makes "the mutator only writes .dox" a sufficient job description.

  The biology receipt

  This is punctuated equilibrium — Gould and Eldredge, look it up: long
  stretches of stasis with parametric drift, then short bursts of structural
  innovation when the environment (the fitness plateau) demands it. The GA
  literature calls the general family memetic algorithms — global structural
  search wrapped around cheap local search. You ain't inventing a weird one-off;
   you're implementing the pattern with a theorem prover as the immune system.
  That last part — that's the novelty.

  Failure modes — 'cause I don't sell without disclosing

  1. The LLM is too good. Called on a plateau, it just... writes the obviously
  best strategy, all M variants converge, diversity dies, and you've got
  design-by-committee-of-one wearing evolution's coat. Mitigation: demand M
  deliberately divergent structural bets (aggressive / economic / defensive),
  and let the dice and the arena pick the winner. The LLM proposes; fitness
  disposes.
  2. Schema bloat. Every extension grows the search space; the inner loop needs
  more evaluations per generation to explore it, or the new genes just sit there
   unexplored while the old optimum keeps winning. Mitigation: prune — genes
  that stay at their default across the whole population for J generations get
  frozen back out of the schema.
  3. Fake plateaus. In coevolution mode fitness is noisy — your score depends on
   what the rival evolved. Stagnation might be an arms-race stalemate, not a
  real optimum. Mitigation: before declaring stasis, re-score the champion in
  the isolated arena (fixed benchmark map, repeated trials). Don't call the
  surgeon 'cause the scale is broken.

  The loop, one breath

  each generation:
    if plateau(archive, K):                      # rare
        pool ← LLM_structural_variants(champion, archive, telemetry)   # schema
  may grow
    else:                                        # common
        pool ← select + crossover + point_mutate(population)
    specs  ← render genome→.dox (schema-driven)
    viable ← paradox check + Z3 obligations + tsc-vs-frozen-shell      # free
  kills
    deploy N accounts → run T ticks → score from Memory.stats
    archive(genome, score, telemetry); keep elites

  Every box in that diagram except two already has scaffolding in this repo: the
   checkers exist, the server is pure, deploy self-provisions, telemetry polls
  over HTTP, nix parallelizes the population. The two missing organs: the
  schema-driven generator and the generic-interpreter shell refactor. Everything
   else is plumbing.

  So — you want the phased plan written down as actual tasks against this repo,
  or you got another angle you want cross-examined first?

  The argument that kills it: the gate already exists

  Walk the failure modes. What happens if the orchestrator is buggy?

  - It produces a malformed genome → dies at the gate. paradox check + Z3 + tsc
  re-verify every artifact downstream, no matter who made it. That was the
  design from day one — the trust boundary is the gate, never the producer. I
  said that about the LLM's output and somehow forgot it applies to the
  orchestrator's output too.
  - It mis-renders, mis-deploys, double-runs → visible in the arena. Scores come
   from raw archived telemetry, which means they're recomputable. Nothing
  silent.
  - It corrupts the archive → append-only file + content hashes. That's a
  file-open mode and a SHA, not a theorem.

  The architecture already has a verification story, and the orchestrator sits
  inside it. Proving the glue correct when everything the glue produces gets
  independently checked is buying a second belt while wearing suspenders made of
   Z3.

  Look at that table. The one property that actually keeps me up at night —
  crash-resume without double-scoring — lives exactly where refinement types
  don't reach. LH would've proven the trivial 70% and shrugged at the hard 30%.

  The one real risk, and the better weapon

  Silent search degradation — elitism quietly dropping the champion, selection
  quietly biased — that's the bug the gate won't catch, 'cause every individual
  artifact is valid while the search rots. My answer last turn was: prove the
  model. Better answer: audit the run. An evolve audit command that replays the
  archive and asserts the invariants over what actually happened — best-so-far
  monotone, generations dense and increasing, every genome's lineage closed,
  scores recomputable from telemetry. Static proof verifies your model of the
  loop; the audit verifies the actual loop, IO warts included. For this system,
  that's strictly more truth per line of code. And it's the same move as the
  itest: don't prove the harvester harvests — watch it harvest.

  What LH would've cost you

  GHC version coupling (the plugin chronically lags releases), nix packaging of
  LH + Z3 into the build, proof-maintenance on every kernel refactor, error
  messages that read like Z3 having a bad day — real, recurring costs, paid by a
   solo dev, for proofs of things a property test covers in ten lines.

  Verdict: plain Haskell. Strong types doing the free work (NonEmpty, newtypes,
  smart constructors), QuickCheck properties on the pure kernel, evolve audit as
   runtime verification over the archive. And here's the exit that makes
  deferring safe: LH is additive — annotations on existing Haskell, not a
  rewrite. If the kernel ever grows an invariant that property tests can't
  corner, the door's still open and nothing needs tearing down.
