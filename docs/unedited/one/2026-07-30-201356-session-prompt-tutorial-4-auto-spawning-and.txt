❯ what is the spec behavior in tutorial 4                                       

● Here's the spec's testimony — what the brain now says, tutorial 4 edition. And
   note what's remarkable: no new states, no new events, no machine changes.
  Turnover was already latent in the spawnQueue policy; section 4 is three
  policy declarations:

  1. Capacity-tiered bodies — workerBodyTiers, richest first

  heavyWorkerBody = [work, work, carry, move, move]   → cost 350
  workerBody      = [work, carry, move]               → cost 200
  workerBodyTiers = [heavyWorkerBody, workerBody]     → ORDER IS THE POLICY

  RoleSpec.body is dead; RoleSpec.bodies is an ordered list, and every role uses
   the same tier list. The shell walks it front to back and spawns the first 
  affordable tier — mechanical maximization, same covenant as extensionOffsets:
  the spec ranks, the world disposes. 350 is not an accident — it's exactly
  spawn (300) + one extension (50), so the top tier spends everything tutorial 3
   built.

  2. Afford basis — spawnAffordBasis = energyAvailable

  The explicit policy fork, decided IN the spec with the rationale written next
  to it: afford against energy on hand, not capacity. Why? Because
  wait-for-capacity is a deadlock dressed up as ambition — all harvesters die,
  the extension never refills, 350 never arrives, and the spawn stands there
  waiting on a promise no one's alive to keep. Afford-now means:

  - Bootstrap / crisis: energy is low → the 200 body spawns immediately. The
  colony recovers.
  - Steady state: sinks are full (tutorial 3 proved it) → the moment a creep
  dies, 350 is sitting there → the heavy body spawns. Capacity gets spent.

  One constant, both behaviors. That's the whole point of putting it in the
  spec.

  3. Population — harvesterSpec.desired: 2

  Section 4's one genuine constant: "maintain at least 2 harvesters." Queue is
  now harvester×2, upgrader×1, builder×2 — five souls, priority still list
  order.

  What tutorial 4 does NOT touch

  CreepEvent, all seven states, all three machines — untouched. Death and
  rebirth are observations, not policy: the shell counts corpses in the memory
  sweep and births at spawnCreep, and the itest asserts the emergent claim — the
   colony refills to 2/1/2, spawns heavy, and keeps upgrading after the funeral.
   The organism outlives its cells without a single new transition. That's the
  spec flexing: turnover was never a feature to add. It was a theorem to prove.
